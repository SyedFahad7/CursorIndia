# Cursor India — Strategy & Positioning

> Phase 5 deliverable.
> Inputs: Cursor brand analysis (§1), ambassador template audit (§2), community site comparison matrix (§3), external inspiration (§4).

This document defines **how Cursor India positions itself, talks, and grows.** The architecture (Phase 6) and design system (Phase 7) are downstream of these decisions.

---

## 1. Strategic Context

### 1.1 The opportunity (numbers and reality)

- **India is the second-largest developer population on Earth** (GitHub reports India will overtake the US as the largest by ~2027).
- **India has ~5,000+ engineering colleges**, ~1.5M engineering graduates annually, and 100K+ active early-career builders.
- **Indian developers index disproportionately high on AI-coding adoption** (multiple internal surveys at Stripe, GitHub, JetBrains; reflected in Cursor's own student testimonials).
- **No Indian city currently appears in the public Cursor `/community` events feed** at time of analysis (Bangladesh and Sri Lanka are listed; India is not). This is a structural gap.
- **The Indian startup ecosystem** is the world's #3 (after US and China) by company count, and the most rapidly AI-pivoting market by funding share.

### 1.2 The void Cursor India fills

| Surface | Owned by | Cursor India's role |
|---|---|---|
| Product website | cursor.com | Refer / direct traffic |
| Global community page | cursor.com/community | Drive India events into this feed |
| Discord (global) | discord.gg/cursor | Surface India-relevant threads |
| Forum (global) | forum.cursor.com | Open and own an India tag |
| **Indian-developer-specific landing, depth, events, ambassador, project showcase, vernacular content, college outreach, startup-founder track, partnership ecosystem** | **nobody** | **Cursor India fills this.** |

### 1.3 The strategic posture

**Cursor India is an *official community outpost*** — recognized by the Cursor Ambassador program, aligned to Cursor brand, run by ambassadors, but organizationally independent (volunteer, community-funded for events, sponsor-funded for scaled programs).

We are **not**:
- Cursor's official India sales team (Cursor's own teams handle enterprise).
- An exclusive club (the community is open by default).
- A reseller, training company, or paid bootcamp.
- A political organization or nationalist project — we serve every Indian developer.

## 2. Positioning

### 2.1 One-sentence positioning

> **Cursor India is where every Indian developer comes to build with AI — together.**

### 2.2 Long-form positioning paragraph

> Cursor India is the community of developers, students, founders, and open-source contributors across India who are building with Cursor. We host Cafe Cursor, workshops, meetups, and hackathons across Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Pune, Chennai, Kolkata, and the growing list of Indian cities where our chapters operate. We're not the official Cursor product team — we're the people who use Cursor every day, contribute to its community, and want to make it easier for the next million Indian developers to ship.

### 2.3 Tagline candidates (short, restrained, brand-aligned)

1. **"Build with Cursor in India."** (recommended — simplest, true, low-key)
2. "India's Cursor community. Built by builders."
3. "Where Indian developers ship with Cursor."
4. "Cursor, every city in India."

> Recommendation: **#1** as primary tagline; use the long-form positioning paragraph as the meta description.

### 2.4 What we are NOT (kill list)

Every brand needs a kill list to stay sharp.

- "India's #1 AI community" — superlative, unprovable, off-brand.
- "Cursor's official Indian partner" — false, opens legal questions.
- Anything with 🇮🇳 in the title or branding.
- "Join the AI revolution" — bro tone.
- "Limited spots — apply now!" — false urgency.
- "Exclusive ambassador club" — closed framing.

## 3. Audience Segments

We serve six primary audiences. All six should find themselves on the homepage within 5 seconds of arriving.

### 3.1 Working developers
- **Who:** Indian software engineers (early to staff), employed at Indian SaaS, services firms, GCCs, FAANG-India, fintech.
- **Need:** Faster, more confident adoption of Cursor in their daily work. Local meetups to network and benchmark.
- **Funnel:** Events → Discord → company-internal champion.

### 3.2 Students
- **Who:** B.Tech, M.Tech, dual-degree, undergrad CS at IITs, IIITs, NITs, BITS, IISc, ISI, IIIT-H, IIIT-B, IIITDM, regional engineering colleges.
- **Need:** Free Cursor (the global `cursor.com/students` program offers this), peer cohort, projects to ship, jobs/internships.
- **Funnel:** Campus Lead program → Campus events → free Cursor → project showcase → internships through partners.

### 3.3 Indie hackers / Solo founders
- **Who:** Bootstrapped, often non-VC-funded builders. Heavily concentrated in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Indore.
- **Need:** Build/ship faster, community accountability, audience for what they're shipping.
- **Funnel:** Hackathons → Project showcase → Cursor for Startups credits → optional partner intros (YC, Surge, etc.).

### 3.4 Startup founders & engineering leaders
- **Who:** YC India, Sequoia/Peak XV, Lightspeed India, Z47, Surge alums. CTOs at series A–C.
- **Need:** Hire AI-native engineers, adopt Cursor team-wide, network with other CTOs.
- **Funnel:** Founders' track events (closed-door) → Cursor for Enterprise intros → recruiting talent from the community.

### 3.5 Open-source contributors
- **Who:** India's strong OSS contributor base — Linux Foundation India, MSR India alumni, package authors.
- **Need:** Recognition, collaboration, paths into AI-native dev tools.
- **Funnel:** OSS showcase → "OSS contributor of the month" recognition → potential ambassador track.

### 3.6 Community organizers / aspiring ambassadors
- **Who:** People already running their own communities (Razorpay FTX, FOSS communities, regional dev groups, GDG, FOSSAsia chapters).
- **Need:** A path to become Cursor Ambassador / Campus Lead / Chapter Co-organizer.
- **Funnel:** Volunteer → Co-organize → Apply → Onboard.

## 4. Brand Voice

### 4.1 Voice attributes

| Attribute | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Calm** | We don't shout. Confidence speaks softly. | "Build with Cursor in Bengaluru. Saturday, 4 pm at Third Wave Coffee Indiranagar." |
| **Specific** | Concrete > vague. Always. | "60 developers shipped 14 projects in 12 hours." not "amazing turnout!" |
| **Technical** | We respect the reader's intelligence. | "We'll deep-dive Composer 2.5 long-horizon behavior." not "AI is amazing!" |
| **Warm** | We're a community, not a brochure. | "Bring your laptop, whatever you're working on, and your favourite snack to share." |
| **Honest** | Acknowledge limits, name partners, credit work. | "We're not the official Cursor team — see cursor.com." |
| **Indian-confident** | Indian English, not anxious mimicry of US tone. | "We organise, we don't 'organize'. We catch up, we don't 'circle back'." |

### 4.2 Do / Don't (writing rules)

**DO**
- Use **Indian English** consistently — but make a style choice (UK-leaning) and document it.
- Name venues, cities, partners, people. Specificity is a brand value.
- Use **second-person** ("you can host a Cafe Cursor in your city").
- Cite Cursor's own canon — Cafe Cursor, Workshops, Meetups, Hackathons.
- Use IST consistently. Always.
- Write recap dates as `Sat, 12 Jul 2026 · 4 pm IST · Indiranagar`.

**DON'T**
- No emojis as voice (a stray 👋 is okay in chat, never in titles).
- No "amazing", "awesome", "incredible", "world-class" without evidence.
- No exclamation marks. (One per page, max.)
- No "click here". CTAs have verbs: "Register for the meetup →".
- No vanity stats. "1020+ members" only if it's true.
- No "#India" or "🇮🇳" in chrome.

### 4.3 Pull-quote bank (sample voice in action)

> "We're 8 cities in. We'd like to be 25 by the end of the year. If your city isn't on this list, that's where you come in."

> "Cursor India is volunteer-run. Nobody here is paid. We do it because shipping with Cursor is a craft worth gathering around."

> "Cafe Cursor Pune is a five-hour Saturday afternoon at a café in Aundh, with strong filter coffee, slow Wi-Fi, and faster builders."

## 5. Navigation Strategy (sets the stage for Phase 6 architecture)

The navigation must make a working developer in Bengaluru, a student in Coimbatore, and a founder in Gurgaon all find their entry inside 5 seconds.

**Top-nav recommendation (final):**

```
Events  ·  Chapters  ·  Ambassadors  ·  Showcase  ·  Resources  ·  Blog  ·  Join
```

- **Events** — calendar + archetype filter (Cafe / Workshop / Meetup / Hackathon).
- **Chapters** — city pages (Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Pune, Chennai, Kolkata, + a "Bring Cursor to your city" CTA).
- **Ambassadors** — directory + lead bios + apply CTA.
- **Showcase** — projects built with Cursor by Indian builders.
- **Resources** — curated learn material, vernacular guides, college playbooks, hackathon kit.
- **Blog** — longform + monthly roundups.
- **Join** — Discord, WhatsApp, Telegram, newsletter, ambassador application.

Rejected nav items (and why):
- "About" — folded into the footer + a small "/about" page.
- "Partners" — surfaced from Chapters and Events; dedicated `/partners` page exists but is not in top nav.
- "Press" — footer only.
- "Donate / Support us" — out of scope at launch (we are volunteer, not 501c3).

## 6. User Journeys

Six canonical journeys map to the six audience segments.

### Journey 1 — Working developer discovers via search
1. Searches "cursor meetup bengaluru" → lands on `/events?city=bengaluru&type=meetup`.
2. Sees next meetup with venue + date + RSVP.
3. RSVPs on Luma (embedded).
4. Joins WhatsApp / Discord from event detail page.
5. Receives reminder email day-before.
6. Attends → posts a recap photo → tagged in next roundup.

### Journey 2 — Student finds Campus Lead path
1. Recommended by a senior at college → visits `/chapters/bengaluru` or `/campus`.
2. Sees Campus Lead spec, application form, list of existing campus clubs (IIITB, IIIT-H, BITS Goa, IIT-M etc.).
3. Applies via form (`/campus/apply`).
4. Receives confirmation; gets onboarded to the Campus Lead Slack/Discord channel.
5. Hosts first campus event using the **hackathon-in-a-box kit** + Cursor credits.

### Journey 3 — Indie hacker submits a project
1. Discovers via Twitter/X mention or roundup → lands on `/showcase`.
2. Reads other featured projects → "Submit yours →".
3. Submits via PR to `content/projects/*.mdx` OR via form.
4. After review, project appears in the next roundup and in the showcase carousel.

### Journey 4 — Startup founder books a session
1. Comes from podcast / referral → lands on `/founders` (sub-page of `/chapters` or top-level).
2. Sees upcoming closed-door founder roundtable, sees CTOs already attending.
3. Books via private RSVP.
4. Optionally introduced to Cursor Enterprise via partner program.

### Journey 5 — OSS contributor gets recognized
1. Maintainer of a popular Indian-origin OSS project mentioned in a roundup.
2. Followers click through to `/showcase/<project>` (deep-link).
3. Project page features GitHub stars, contributor info, "thanks" section, link to project's repo.

### Journey 6 — Aspiring ambassador applies
1. Volunteered at 2+ events → visible in event recap photos.
2. Visits `/ambassadors/apply`.
3. Provides portfolio, city, why-they-want, how they'd organize.
4. Application reviewed by India lead + at least one existing ambassador.
5. Onboarded; gets a `chapters/<city>` page co-edit access.

## 7. Community Growth Strategy

### 7.1 Year-1 targets (realistic, not vanity)

| Metric | Q1 (M1–3) | Q2 (M4–6) | Q3 (M7–9) | Q4 (M10–12) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Active city chapters | 4 | 8 | 12 | 18 |
| Ambassadors | 6 | 12 | 20 | 30 |
| Campus Leads | 0 | 10 | 25 | 50 |
| Events held (cumulative) | 8 | 30 | 80 | 150 |
| Recaps published | 4 | 20 | 60 | 120 |
| Monthly roundups | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 |
| Newsletter subscribers | 500 | 2,500 | 7,500 | 20,000 |
| WhatsApp community | 200 | 1,200 | 4,000 | 12,000 |
| Discord members | 300 | 1,500 | 5,000 | 15,000 |
| Featured projects | 6 | 20 | 50 | 100 |

These are **honest** targets (no inflation), benchmarked against Sri Lanka's 1020+ members in their first year at smaller country scale, and Build Club's 3000+ at multi-year global scale.

### 7.2 Launch sequence

**Pre-launch (Week −4 to 0):**
- Recruit founding 6 ambassadors across Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Pune, Chennai.
- Each commits to host one event in the first month.
- Set up WhatsApp + Discord + Twitter/X + LinkedIn.
- Build the website (this project).
- Get domain (`cursorindia.dev` recommended — clean, developer-coded TLD).

**Launch (Week 0):**
- Publish the website.
- Announce on Cursor forum (`/c/events`), cursor.com Discord, ambassador's personal channels.
- First event in Bengaluru same week as launch.

**Month 1:**
- 4 events across 4 cities.
- First monthly roundup.
- First Project Showcase entry.
- Apply to be featured on cursor.com/community feed.

**Month 2–3:**
- Add Pune, Chennai chapters.
- First hackathon (Bengaluru, 24h, partner-sponsored prizes).
- First campus event at IIIT Bangalore or IIT Bombay.

**Month 4–6:**
- Scale to 8 chapters.
- Launch Campus Lead program.
- First founders' roundtable (closed, Bengaluru).
- First vernacular content piece (Hindi or Tamil).

### 7.3 Growth channels (ranked by leverage)

1. **Ambassador networks** — each ambassador's existing community amplifies launch.
2. **College Campus Leads** — by Q3, the largest single growth vector.
3. **Twitter/X + LinkedIn** — Indian dev Twitter is strong; ambassadors should post recaps natively.
4. **Cursor.com/community feed** — once events list, organic global discovery.
5. **Partner amplification** — every venue partner, every sponsor, every co-host shares.
6. **Word-of-mouth at events** — every Cafe Cursor adds 5–10 attendees to Discord/WhatsApp.
7. **YouTube talk recordings** — long-tail SEO + repeat engagement.
8. **Vernacular content** — unlocks Tier-2/3 cities and non-English-first developers.
9. **GitHub README** — link from our website's repo to every event and recap. Public-by-default.
10. **Newsletter referral** — once subscriber base is real, referral mechanics.

### 7.5 Funding model

Cursor India is **volunteer-run**. Cost categories:

- **Free / volunteer:** website hosting (Vercel free), Discord, WhatsApp, GitHub, Luma free tier, ambassador time.
- **Per-event sponsor-covered:** venue (often free via partner), food & drinks (sponsored), prizes (sponsored or Cursor credits).
- **Centrally needed (small):** domain (~$10/yr), Pagefind/Algolia free tier, Plausible self-host or ~$10/mo, Resend free tier, OG asset design.

We can run the entire program for **< ₹50,000 / year** in central infrastructure if we keep discipline.

## 8. Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bus factor on the lead organizer | Medium | High | Always co-leads per chapter. Volunteer Team layer. |
| Brand drift (someone goes off-tone) | Medium | High | Brand voice doc, review process, single editorial owner. |
| Discord/WhatsApp drama | Medium | Medium | Code of Conduct, moderator team, escalation process. |
| Naming confusion (people think we are Cursor Inc.) | High | Medium | Disambiguation FAQ on every page (mirror Cursor Germany). |
| Single-city dominance | High | Medium | Multi-chapter from Day 1. Featured rotation across chapters. |
| Sponsorship over-commitment | Medium | High | Partner tier doc; no exclusive deals at launch. |
| Vernacular content quality | Medium | Medium | Native-speaker volunteer review. |
| Event safety / venue incidents | Low | High | Insurance per event (optional), Code of Conduct enforced. |
| Diversity / inclusion gaps | Medium | High | Explicit diversity goals; Women in Cursor India track; accessibility-first events. |
| Cursor brand misalignment getting noticed by Cursor team | Low | High | Keep tone calm and aligned; communicate openly with Cursor community team. |
